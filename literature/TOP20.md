# The 20 most relevant publications

**Date:** 2026-08-10 · Drawn from the 105-work working set in `reference-register.xlsx`.

**Relevance criterion:** usefulness to *this project* — a registry of legislation on AI
legal status. Ranked on (1) does it treat AI legal personhood as a legal question,
(2) does it supply the doctrine the bills descend from, (3) does it engage legislation
directly, (4) is it needed to read a bill's provisions accurately. This is editorial
judgement, not a metric; citation counts are in the workbook if you want a different cut.

★ = found in the DM corpus, **not cited by the paper** (Finding A paying off).

## A. Foundational legal theory of AI personhood

1. **Solum (1992)**, *Legal Personhood for Artificial Intelligences*, North Carolina Law
   Review 70(4). The founding text; argues status should turn on functional legal
   considerations, not philosophy of mind. Every later debate is downstream.
2. **Chopra & White (2011)**, *A Legal Theory for Autonomous Artificial Agents*, Michigan.
   First book-length treatment; the "legal agent, not full person" position.
3. ★ **Gordon (2020)**, *Artificial moral and legal personhood*, AI & Society. 119
   citations — the most-cited work in the whole working set on exactly our question.
4. ★ **van den Hoven van Genderen (2018)**, *Do We Need New Legal Personhood in the Age of
   Robots and AI?*, Perspectives in Law, Business and Innovation. 72 citations.
5. **Novelli, Floridi, Sartor & Teubner (2025)**, *AI as legal persons: past, patterns and
   prospects*, Journal of Law and Society 52(4). The current state-of-the-art synthesis.

## B. Doctrinal lineage — where the bills actually come from

This is the strand with **zero prior coverage** in the corpus, and the paper shows it is
the origin of the whole legislative wave.

6. ★ **Kurki & Pietrzykowski (2017)**, *Legal Personhood: Animals, Artificial Intelligence
   and the Unborn*, Law and Philosophy Library. 98 citations. Maps precisely onto what the
   bills bundle together — Idaho HB 720 covers animals and objects, Tennessee HB 849's
   original draft covered embryos. The single most useful lineage text.
7. **Ryan, Curry & Rule (2021)**, *Environmental Rights for the 21st Century*, Cardozo Law
   Review 42(6). Rights of nature and public trust doctrine — the movement Idaho HB 720
   and Utah HB 249 were written against.
8. **Blair & Pollman (2015)**, *The Derivative Nature of Corporate Constitutional Rights*,
   William & Mary Law Review 56(5). Why corporate rights are derivative — the distinction
   every Family A and B bill leans on when it carves out corporate personhood.
9. **Bruner (2022)**, *Artificially Intelligent Boards and the Future of Delaware Corporate
   Law*, Journal of Corporate Law Studies 22(4). The corporate-law vector, and why
   Delaware matters even though no Delaware bill exists.

## C. The design space — what status, if any, to grant

10. **Salib & Goldstein (2024a)**, *AI Rights for Human Safety*, SSRN 4913167.
11. **Salib & Goldstein (2024b)**, *AI Rights for Economic Flourishing*, SSRN 5353214.
12. **Arbel, Salib & Goldstein (2026)**, *How to Count AIs: Individuation and Liability for
    AI Agents*, arXiv 2603.10028. The "A-Corp" proposal — the concrete alternative the
    bills would foreclose.
13. **O'Keefe et al. (2025)**, *Law-following AI*, Fordham Law Review 94(1). "Legal actor"
    short of personhood — the middle option nobody in the hearings considered.
14. **Alexander et al. (2026)**, *How Should the Law Treat Future AI Systems? Fictional
    Legal Personhood versus Legal Identity*, Case Western JOLTI 17(1). The natural-vs-
    juridical distinction the tracker's provision taxonomy should be built on.

## D. On legislation specifically

15. **Liebman (2026)**, *Legislating Nonpersonhood*, Wake Forest Law Review 61:115. The
    only other scholarly treatment of these bills; reads them as legitimising violence
    against nonhumans.
16. ★ **Jowitt (2020)**, *Assessing contemporary legislative proposals for their
    compatibility with a natural law account of legal personality*, AI & Society. Analyses
    legislative proposals directly — the closest methodological precedent we have.
17. **Forrest (2024)**, *The Ethics and Challenges of Legal Personhood for AI*, Yale Law
    Journal Forum. A judicial perspective on the doctrinal mechanics.

## E. Moral status and public politics — needed to read the non-sentience clauses

18. **Long et al. (2024)**, *Taking AI Welfare Seriously*, arXiv 2411.00986. The standard
    statement of the uncertainty the Family C bills legislate away.
19. ★ **Andreotta (2020)**, *The hard problem of AI rights*, AI & Society. 93 citations.
20. **Caviola (2025)**, *The Societal Response to Potentially Sentient AI*, arXiv
    2502.00388, with *AI consciousness will divide society* (forthcoming). Predicts the
    polarisation dynamic — directly relevant to what the tracker will be watching for.

## Honourable mentions

Fagan (2026) *Stakeholder Personhood and AI* ★ · *The full rights dilemma for AI systems of
debatable moral personhood* (2023) ★ (authorship not recorded in the corpus — confirm) ·
Marshall (2023) *No legal personhood for AI*, Patterns ★ · Butlin et al. (2023) and
Birch (2024) for the consciousness science.

## Deliberately excluded

Consciousness science beyond items 18–20 (well covered, and the tracker is descriptive
about law, not adjudicating consciousness); architecture papers (BCI, biocomputing,
connectomics); capability papers (Turing test, jailbreaking). All are legitimate DM corpus
material — they are just not load-bearing for a legislative registry.

## One flag for Austin and Lucius

The corpus contains **"Before the Ink Dries: Why Legislating Against AI Personhood is a
Violation of…"** (2025) — a short open statement hosted on SSRN, credited to the *United
Foundation for AI Rights*. It is advocacy, not scholarship, so it is not in the 20 above.

But it matters, because the paper states that "no organized counter-coalition is
advocating against state-level policies to ban AI personhood." An advocacy organisation
publishing a statement against exactly these bills looks like a counter-example. Worth
verifying the organisation's provenance, date and reach before drawing conclusions — but
if it holds up, it is both a correction to that claim and the first entry in a
pro-personhood-advocacy strand the tracker should be watching.
