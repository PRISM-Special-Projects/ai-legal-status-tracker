# Provision tags — operational definitions

A tag is applied only if the **text** of the version being tagged satisfies the test below.
Two readers applying these tests to the same text should reach the same answer. If they would
not, the test is defective and should be reported.

Tags describe **textual features**, not policy strength. Presence of a tag is not a judgement
about whether a provision is good, broad, effective or wise.

Every entry gives a positive test, at least one **negative** example, and what it excludes.
The negative examples matter most: they are where the Family C confusion came from.

---

## `denies_legal_personhood`
**Test.** The text states that an AI system may not be granted, or may not be recognised as
having, legal personhood or the status of a person.
**Positive.** "No AI system shall be granted the status of a person or any form of legal
personhood." · "'Person' does not include artificial intelligence."
**Negative.** "An AI system is not an entity capable of bearing liability" — that is
`bars_ai_liability`, a narrower claim about one incident of personality.
**Excludes.** Bars on specific incidents (property, marriage, office) without a general
personhood statement.

## `declares_non_sentient`
**Test.** The text asserts as a matter of law that AI does not possess sentience,
consciousness, self-awareness or similar traits.
**Positive.** "AI systems are declared to be nonsentient entities for all purposes."
**Negative.** A bill that denies personhood but says nothing about mental properties.
**Excludes.** Restrictions on what a chatbot may *claim* about itself — that is
`restricts_chatbot_claims`.

## `assigns_liability_to_humans`
**Test.** The text affirmatively allocates liability for AI-caused harm to a human or
corporate actor.
**Positive.** "Any direct or indirect harm caused by an AI system's operation shall be the
responsibility of the owner or user who directed or employed the AI."
**Negative.** "Artificial intelligence is not a person against which liability may be
imposed" — that removes liability from the AI without allocating it to anyone. **Wisconsin
AB 959 is exactly this case, and tagging it `assigns_liability_to_humans` would have been
wrong.**
**Negative.** Attributing *assets* generated by an AI to its developer — that is
`attributes_ai_assets_to_humans`, an ownership rule, not a liability rule.

## `bars_ai_liability`
**Test.** The text states that liability may not be imposed on the AI system itself.
**Positive.** "An AI system is not an entity capable of bearing liability in its own right."
**Negative.** A provision allocating liability to a human, absent any statement about the AI.
**Note.** This and `assigns_liability_to_humans` are independent. A bill may do either, both,
or neither. Missouri HB 1462 does both; Wisconsin AB 959 does only this.

## `addresses_corporate_veil`
**Test.** The text expressly addresses piercing the corporate veil, or the liability of parent
or controlling entities, in connection with AI.
**Positive.** Both the Missouri formulation (courts *may* pierce on listed conditions) and the
Ohio formulation (parents are immune *unless* those conditions are shown) satisfy it.
**Note.** The tag records that the subject is addressed, **not** the direction of the rule.
Direction is recorded in the record's notes and is visible in the text. Do not read tag
presence as agreement between bills.

## `bars_marriage_or_union` · `bars_property_ownership` · `bars_corporate_office`
**Test.** The text expressly prohibits the named status for an AI system.
**Excludes.** A general personhood denial from which the prohibition might be *inferred*. The
text must address it.

## `imposes_safety_duties`
**Test.** The text places an affirmative duty of oversight, supervision, risk assessment or
safety mechanism on a human or corporate actor.
**Negative.** A statement that failure to supervise "may constitute negligence" *without* an
affirmative duty. Read the whole provision.

## `incident_reporting_duty`
**Test.** The text requires notification of authorities following a defined class of incident.

## `provides_compliance_safe_harbor`
**Test.** The text deems a duty satisfied, or bars a category of liability, where a named
standard or process is followed.
**Positive.** Missouri's HCS: oversight is demonstrated by adherence to NIST AI 100-1.

## `restricts_chatbot_claims`
**Test.** The text restricts what an AI system may assert to a user about its own nature.
**Positive.** "Claiming that the companion chatbot is sentient, conscious, or human."
**Negative.** A legal declaration that AI is non-sentient — that is `declares_non_sentient`,
which binds courts, not chatbots.

## `restricts_person_like_training`
**Test.** The text restricts training an AI to behave in person-like ways.
**Positive.** Tennessee HB 1455 as introduced: training AI to "provide emotional support" or
"act as a companion".
**Note.** Applies to no operative text in the registry; it survives only on the introduced
version of the Tennessee bill. This is intended, not an error.

## `restricts_ai_speech_rights`
**Test.** The text excludes AI from free-expression protection.

## `covers_non_ai_entities`
**Test.** The text extends the same prohibition to non-AI entities such as animals, bodies of
water or inanimate objects.

## `study_only`
**Test.** The text directs a study or report and creates no operative prohibition.

## `defines_human_to_include_unborn`
**Test.** The text defines "human being" or an equivalent term to include the unborn.
**Note.** Present only on Tennessee HB 849 **as introduced**. Recorded because it was removed
in committee, and a registry that tagged only operative text would lose it entirely.

## `creates_criminal_offence`
**Test.** The text makes defined conduct a criminal offence.
**Note.** Present only on Tennessee HB 1455 as introduced.

## `creates_private_right_of_action`
**Test.** The text creates a civil cause of action for private parties.
**Note.** Present only on Tennessee HB 1455 as introduced.

## Rules

1. Tag the **version**, not the bill. A record's headline tags describe its operative text; the
   history lives on the versions.
2. If a tag requires reading intent rather than text, it is the wrong tag.
3. `not_assessed` is different from absent. Absent means checked and not present.
4. Adding a tag requires adding an entry here in the same commit. CI enforces this.
5. One heading per test. A heading may name several keys only where they share a single
   `**Test.**` line, as the three status bars do. Keys with distinct tests get their own
   heading, because `audit/make_sheets.py` quotes each key's test into the audit sheets and
   a "respectively" list cannot be split back into its keys reliably.
